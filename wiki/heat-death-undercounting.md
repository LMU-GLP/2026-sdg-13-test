# Heat Deaths Are Undercounted — Two Sources, Same Pattern

Sources: [EPA heat-related deaths indicator](../sources/epa-heat-related-deaths-indicator-2024.md),
[LA County DPH data brief](../sources/la-county-dph-extreme-heat-data-brief-2025.md).

Two independent sources — one national, one county-level — both conclude
that official heat-death counts are well below the real toll. Per CLAUDE.md
rule 3, both are kept here rather than averaged into one number, since they
measure different populations with different methods.

## The national picture (EPA / CDC)

EPA's technical documentation for its national heat-death indicator states
plainly: "this indicator very likely underestimates the number of deaths
caused by exposure to heat," because death certificates only capture heat
as an explicit cause, not as a contributing stressor behind a heart attack
or respiratory death
([EPA heat-related deaths indicator](../sources/epa-heat-related-deaths-indicator-2024.md)).
Quantified: "some statistical approaches estimate that more than 1,300
deaths per year in the United States are due to extreme heat, compared with
about 750 deaths per year" in the official underlying-and-contributing-cause
dataset — the modeled estimate is roughly 1.7x the official count
([EPA heat-related deaths indicator](../sources/epa-heat-related-deaths-indicator-2024.md)).

The 1995 Chicago heat wave is EPA's own worked example: researchers found
"692 deaths in excess of the background death rate" (approximately 700 for
Cook County specifically, July 11–27), while "only 465 deaths were
attributed to extreme heat on death certificates during that period" —
official records caught about two-thirds of the modeled excess
([EPA heat-related deaths indicator](../sources/epa-heat-related-deaths-indicator-2024.md)).

## The LA County picture

LA County's own data brief flags the same gap at a local scale: official
mortality was "125 deaths ... due to heat-related illness from 2015 to
2024," but a 2024 Medical Examiner study found deaths rose "7%, 15% and 66%
on days with moderate, major, and extreme HeatRisk" versus no-risk days,
estimating "elevated heat may have led to as many as 1,265 additional
deaths between 2014-2019" alone — a single five-year window's modeled
estimate that dwarfs the decade's official count
([LA County DPH data brief](../sources/la-county-dph-extreme-heat-data-brief-2025.md)).
The county brief also notes its own figures sit "well below those for
inland areas such as Phoenix, Arizona (approximately 118, 213 and 238
heat-caused deaths in 2018, 2020, and 2022, respectively)" — cited here as
the county's own comparison, not one we're constructing (see
[LA vs. South Korea comparison](la-vs-south-korea-comparison.md) on why
cross-region comparisons need care).

## Why this matters for the challenge

Both sources agree on direction (official counts are too low) but disagree
on scale and method — EPA's national estimate is built from modeled
excess-mortality statistics, LA's from a Medical Examiner HeatRisk-day
analysis. Neither figure translates directly to South Korea, which counts
differently again (see
[South Korea extreme heat](south-korea-extreme-heat.md)). A neighborhood
project that wants a "how many people are actually affected" number should
treat the official count as a floor, not an estimate.
