# Challenge framing

> **How might we help a neighborhood stay safe during extreme heat?**

## Our angle (from the 2026-07-29 kickoff)

- **Who:** [elderly residents](people/elderly-residents.md). Older adults account for the
  majority of heat deaths in most countries, and they are the group most likely to be
  isolated, on fixed incomes, and living without (or not using) cooling. *[unsourced]*
- **Where:** undecided between [Los Angeles](regions/los-angeles.md) and
  [South Korea](regions/south-korea.md). See the [region comparison](region-comparison.md)
  for how we'll choose.
- **Scale:** a *neighborhood*, not a city or nation. We care about interventions that work
  at the block/community level, even if data comes from higher levels.
- **Stance:** problem-first. We are not committed to any solution type yet. The wiki's job
  is to make the problem legible; solutions come after the research is in.

## What "safe" might mean

Working definition to refine as sources come in: fewer heat-related deaths and
hospitalizations among elderly residents during heat events, achieved through some mix of
prevention (cooler homes), protection (access to cooling), and response (someone notices
when a person is in trouble). *[unsourced]*
