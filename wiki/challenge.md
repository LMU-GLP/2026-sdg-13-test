# The challenge

**How might we help a neighborhood stay safe during extreme heat?**
(SDG 13: Climate Action — GLP 2026)

## Our focus: elderly residents

We chose to center elderly residents. Heat is broadly dangerous, but older
people carry the highest mortality risk, are more likely to live alone, and
are hardest to reach through ordinary alerts. *(uncited — this is our
working hypothesis from the kickoff interview; the first sources must
confirm or correct it for each region.)*

## How we'll pick a region (the decision test)

Agreed in the kickoff interview, 2026-07-29. We commit to the region where:

1. **Harm is worst** — heat deaths, ER visits, and vulnerability among
   elderly residents.
2. **The gap is clearest** — existing programs (cooling centers, alert
   systems, check-in schemes) are demonstrably failing to reach elderly
   people, so a new idea adds something.
3. **The data exists** — we can find solid, citable, ideally
   neighborhood-level evidence to defend the pitch.

A region wins by scoring well on all three, not by enthusiasm.

## Solution direction

Deliberately open. Candidate directions we surfaced (community check-in
networks, cooling access, early warning that actually activates elderly
people) are noted in [research-plan.md](research-plan.md), but we pick one
only after the evidence is in.
