# Problem statement

Adopted 2026-07-29, from the team decision session. Region committed:
**Los Angeles** — rationale recorded in
[region-comparison.md](region-comparison.md).

In South Los Angeles, extreme heat kills elderly residents quietly,
indoors, in homes that never make the news. A sustained indoor
temperature of just 85°F can become deadly within a day or two
([ABC7, 2024](../sources/abc7-socal-heat-deaths-undercounted-2024.md)),
and South LA is where that danger concentrates: it has more homes
without air conditioning than anywhere else in the county, Leimert Park
logs more than double the heat-related hospital visits of neighboring
Culver City, and historically redlined areas show very high rates of
heat-related injuries
([ABC7, 2024](../sources/abc7-socal-heat-deaths-undercounted-2024.md)).
Older adults are among those at greatest risk — more sensitive to heat,
more likely to have chronic conditions or take medications that impair
the body's response to it
([LA County DPH, 2025](../sources/la-county-dph-extreme-heat-data-brief-2025.md);
[LAist, 2024](../sources/laist-ladwp-free-ac-seniors-2024.md)).

The true scale is hidden. LA County officially recorded 125 heat deaths
across 2015–2024, but its own Medical Examiner analysis estimates heat
may have contributed to as many as 1,265 additional deaths in 2014–2019
alone — because when an elderly person dies of kidney failure or heart
disease during a heat wave, the death certificate rarely says "heat"
([LA County DPH, 2025](../sources/la-county-dph-extreme-heat-data-brief-2025.md)).
Invisible deaths attract no budgets, and the help that exists today is
small and effortful: roughly 500 free AC units distributed in a summer,
to seniors who must hold utility paperwork and physically queue at a
senior center
([LAist, 2024](../sources/laist-ladwp-free-ac-seniors-2024.md)) —
against thousands of heat-illness ER visits a year and a heat season now
46 days longer than in the 1960s
([LA County DPH, 2025](../sources/la-county-dph-extreme-heat-data-brief-2025.md)).

**How might we make staying cool at home the default for South LA's
low-income seniors — rather than something they must discover, qualify
for, and travel to claim?**
