# Los Angeles vs. South Korea

The team hasn't picked a region. This page exists to compare what the
sources actually say, not to average them together.

## Why the headline death counts can't be compared

- **LA County:** 125 officially recorded heat deaths, 2015–2024, in one
  U.S. county — and the county's own Medical Examiner analysis suggests the
  true toll over a shorter, overlapping window (2014–2019) may be as high
  as 1,265 ([source](../sources/la-extreme-heat.md)).
- **South Korea:** 238 heat deaths recorded nationally since 2011, by
  diagnosis, per KDCA monitoring ([source](../sources/korea-heat-deaths-elderly.md)).

The Korea source is explicit that these two figures should not be set side
by side: "Korea counts deaths where heat illness was the diagnosis,
nationally, since 2011. The LA brief counts recorded heat deaths in one
county and then says its own figure is probably far too low. The two are
not measuring the same thing at the same scale"
([source](../sources/korea-heat-deaths-elderly.md)). Different geography
(one county vs. one country), different time windows, and different
counting methods (official diagnosis vs. death-certificate undercounting
that the county itself flags). Per this wiki's citation rules, that
disqualifies a direct comparison.

## What genuinely is comparable

**Who is most at risk.** Both regions' sources name older adults:

- Korea: 65%+ of recorded deaths are age 60+
  ([source](../sources/korea-heat-deaths-elderly.md)).
- LA: the county brief names older adults among its highest-risk groups,
  but gives no matching percentage breakdown
  ([source](../sources/la-extreme-heat.md)) — so the *direction* agrees,
  but the LA source doesn't support a number as specific as Korea's 65%.

**The trend, not the count.** Both regions show heat harm increasing over
time in their own data:

- LA: heat season is 46 days longer than the 1960s, and deaths jumped from
  34 (2005–2014) to 125 (2015–2024) — a rise within the same county's own
  counting method ([source](../sources/la-extreme-heat.md)).
- Korea: this source is a single point-in-time report and doesn't include a
  multi-year trend line, so no equivalent trend claim can be made from it
  alone ([source](../sources/korea-heat-deaths-elderly.md)).

## Where they differ in kind, not just scale

- **Setting.** Korea's deaths cluster in agricultural, outdoor, often-solo
  work (32% on farms) ([source](../sources/korea-heat-deaths-elderly.md)).
  LA's brief doesn't break its deaths down by setting at all
  ([source](../sources/la-extreme-heat.md)) — that's a gap, not a finding
  that LA's deaths happen elsewhere.
- **What's already running.** LA County has a named set of existing
  programs — text/email alerts, a schools toolkit, a Heat Ambassador
  initiative, a County Heat Action Plan
  ([source](../sources/la-extreme-heat.md)). The Korea source names no
  equivalent program, only official advice to avoid midday outdoor work
  ([source](../sources/korea-heat-deaths-elderly.md)).

## What this means for picking a region

A team choosing LA inherits a documented, order-of-magnitude undercount
problem and an existing program landscape to build alongside or
differentiate from. A team choosing South Korea inherits a sharper,
better-quantified at-risk population (elderly, agricultural, often alone)
but no existing local program in this wiki's sources to react to — and
would need a new source before a solution could be pitched as
non-redundant. Neither region has neighborhood-level data yet in either
source.
