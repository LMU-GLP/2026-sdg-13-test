# Extreme Heat in Los Angeles

Sources: [LA County DPH data brief](../sources/la-county-dph-extreme-heat-data-brief-2025.md),
[Cool Streets LA program](../sources/la-cool-streets-cool-pavement-program.md).

## What LA County's own data shows

LA County defines an extreme heat day locally as a max temperature of
90°F or higher in downtown LA
([LA County DPH data brief](../sources/la-county-dph-extreme-heat-data-brief-2025.md)).
Against that baseline:

- Emergency-department visits for heat-related illness (HRI), 2005–2022,
  "ranged from a low of 456 in 2005 to a high of 1,352 in 2018, with an
  overall average of 898 per year"; 1,195 in 2022. Hospital admissions
  "ranged from 96 in 2011 to 461 in 2018, averaging 196 per year"; 338 in
  2022. Both show "an increasing trend."
  ([LA County DPH data brief](../sources/la-county-dph-extreme-heat-data-brief-2025.md))
- Official mortality: "125 deaths occurred due to heat-related illness from
  2015 to 2024," with only "34 deaths ... recorded in all of 2005–2014" —
  a marked jump.
  ([LA County DPH data brief](../sources/la-county-dph-extreme-heat-data-brief-2025.md))
- The county flags its own undercount: a 2024 Medical Examiner study found
  deaths rose "7%, 15% and 66% on days with moderate, major, and extreme
  HeatRisk" versus no-risk days, and estimated "elevated heat may have led
  to as many as 1,265 additional deaths between 2014-2019" — far above the
  125 officially logged for 2015–2024.
  ([LA County DPH data brief](../sources/la-county-dph-extreme-heat-data-brief-2025.md);
  see also [heat-death undercounting](heat-death-undercounting.md), which
  finds the same pattern nationally.)
- Older adults (65+) are named among the groups "at greater risk of HRI."
  ([LA County DPH data brief](../sources/la-county-dph-extreme-heat-data-brief-2025.md))
- The heat season is stretching: "the average time from the first to the
  last heat wave of the season has increased by 46 days since the 1960s."
  Yet July has the most ER visits (4,020 total, 2005–2022) even though
  August–September have more extreme-heat days — early-season heat hits
  unacclimatized bodies hardest.
  ([LA County DPH data brief](../sources/la-county-dph-extreme-heat-data-brief-2025.md))

## What's already being done

The county brief lists heat alerts (text/email subscription), a school
toolkit, a Heat Ambassador community-health-worker initiative (LARC-HEAT
grant), and the County Heat Action Plan
([LA County DPH data brief](../sources/la-county-dph-extreme-heat-data-brief-2025.md)).

The city (not county) runs **Cool Streets LA**, a StreetsLA program that
coats asphalt with reflective sealant. As of this reporting it has covered
"over 175 lane miles across Los Angeles, covering nearly 600 street
segments since it started in 2017," funded $2–4 million per fiscal year
from a mix of the Street Damage Restoration Fee and state Senate Bill 1
money. It cools pavement "by between 4 to 8 degrees" depending on wear
([Cool Streets LA program](../sources/la-cool-streets-cool-pavement-program.md)).
A UCLA study cited in the same report complicates this, though: reflective
pavement "might feel hotter" to a person standing on it, because the
re-radiated heat increases ambient warmth even as the surface itself reads
cooler
([Cool Streets LA program](../sources/la-cool-streets-cool-pavement-program.md)).

## Open question for a neighborhood project

Neither LA source gives a neighborhood-level (as opposed to countywide or
citywide) breakdown of who is dying, or where Cool Streets segments
overlap with the highest-ED-visit areas. That link is not established in
these sources — it would need a new source if the team wants to target a
specific neighborhood.
