# Pitch script

~90 seconds at a natural speaking pace. Read it out loud once before Monday —
this is the version to memorize the shape of, not read word-for-word.

---

Five people. That's how many elderly farmers died working alone in the heat
this year, in South Korea — just through mid-September, out of twenty-nine
heat deaths total this season. And it's not new. It's a pattern going back
to 2011: two out of three of Korea's recorded heat deaths are people 60 or
older, and nearly a third of them die on farms.

This is for farmers like the two women our research found — one in her
80s, collapsed near a greenhouse in Jinju; another found dead in a rice
paddy on Jeju Island. Both worked alone. Korea's own advice is to stay
inside between noon and five — advice for someone who has somebody else to
cover for them. These farmers don't.

So we built HeatWatch: an early-warning and check-in service. Days before a
heat wave, it calls or texts the farmer, a family member we call their
buddy, and a local health worker — all at once. During the dangerous
hours, the farmer checks in by phone or a simple field device. Miss a
check-in, and the buddy and health worker are alerted immediately.

We built a live page for it — every number on it links straight back to
where we found it.

What we want is one village and one agricultural co-op willing to run a
real pilot with us. Give us one season, and we'll show you whether this
saves a life.
