# Concept: undercounting of heat deaths

Heat kills through other diagnoses. When an elderly person dies of
kidney failure, dehydration, or a heart attack during a heat wave, the
death certificate often never mentions heat — so official heat-death
counts understate the real toll, and the size of the problem stays
politically invisible. This is central to our challenge: the population
we care about is the one most likely to die uncounted.

## Evidence in Los Angeles

- LA County's official count for summer 2024 was 2 heat deaths, versus
  24 in far-smaller Riverside County. UCLA's Dr. David Eisenman: "LA
  County has many millions more people than the Inland Empire, and even
  though it's not as hot here, we would expect to have more than just
  two per year." Deaths from kidney failure or dehydration often aren't
  officially linked to heat
  ([ABC7, 2024](../sources/abc7-socal-heat-deaths-undercounted-2024.md)).
- The county's own data brief concedes its 125 recorded deaths
  (2015–2024) "may underestimate the true local impact of heat on
  mortality," comparing itself unfavorably to Phoenix's counts
  (~118–238 per year in peak years). A Medical Examiner analysis found
  deaths rose "7%, 15% and 66% on days with moderate, major, and
  extreme HeatRisk" and that heat "may have led to as many as 1,265
  additional deaths between 2014-2019" — roughly ten times the official
  decade count
  ([LA County DPH, 2025](../sources/la-county-dph-extreme-heat-data-brief-2025.md)).

## Contrast: South Korea

- Korea's KDCA has run dedicated heat-illness death monitoring since
  2011 with age and location-of-incident breakdowns
  ([Korea Times/Yonhap, 2025](../sources/korea-times-heat-deaths-elderly-2025.md)).
  Its totals (238 deaths since 2011) look small next to LA's excess-death
  estimates, which could mean either less harm or a similar
  certification problem — we have no Korean excess-mortality source yet
  to tell which. *(uncited — open question in

## Why it matters for the pitch

- **Harm test:** raw official numbers will understate LA's elderly heat
  harm; excess-mortality estimates are the honest denominator.
- **Gap test:** a problem that goes uncounted attracts no budget —
  undercounting *is* part of the gap.
- **Data test:** any comparison between regions must compare like with
  like (official counts vs excess-death estimates), never mix the two
  in one number.
