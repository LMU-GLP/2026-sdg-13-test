# Overview: Neighborhood Safety in Extreme Heat

**Challenge:** How might we help a neighborhood stay safe during extreme
heat?

This wiki is built from five sources — two on Los Angeles, two on South
Korea/US national data respectively, and one on the UN policy framework.
See [index](index.md) for the full catalog.

## Where this sits in the SDGs

The challenge maps to SDG Target 13.1 ("strengthen resilience and adaptive
capacity to climate-related hazards"), not to any target that names heat
specifically — Goal 13 doesn't have one. Full breakdown:
[SDG Goal 13 and our challenge](climate-action-goal-13.md).

## The core problem, in both candidate regions

- **Los Angeles**: official county mortality of 125 heat deaths
  (2015–2024), against ER visits averaging 898/year and a Medical
  Examiner estimate of up to 1,265 additional deaths in 2014–2019 alone.
  Full detail: [LA extreme heat](la-extreme-heat.md).
- **South Korea**: 238 cumulative heat deaths since national monitoring
  began in 2011, over 65% of them aged 60+. Full detail:
  [South Korea extreme heat](south-korea-extreme-heat.md).
- These two figures are **not directly comparable** — different
  geography, time window, and age brackets. See
  [LA vs. South Korea comparison](la-vs-south-korea-comparison.md) before
  using either number to argue for a region.

## A pattern that shows up everywhere we looked

Whichever region and whichever dataset, official heat-death counts appear
to substantially undercount the real toll — true in LA County's own
Medical Examiner analysis and in EPA's national indicator documentation.
See [heat-death undercounting](heat-death-undercounting.md).

## What's already being tried

LA runs a funded, measurable pavement-cooling program (Cool Streets LA)
and several county programs without measured outcomes in our sources;
Korea's only sourced intervention is behavioral guidance for outdoor
workers; the one intervention in our source set with a clear
before/after mortality number is Milwaukee's 1995 heat-response plan
(cited inside the EPA documentation, not an LA or Korea example). Full
detail: [interventions and programs](interventions-and-programs.md).

## Where the sources run out

- No source in this wiki names a specific neighborhood (as opposed to
  county- or nation-wide figures) — a neighborhood-scoped project will
  need to find or generate that data itself.
- No source quantifies the outcomes of LA County's alert system, school
  toolkit, or Heat Ambassador program, or of Korea's outdoor-work
  guidance.
- No source gives South Korea an independent excess-mortality estimate the
  way LA and the US national data have one, so we don't know how much
  Korea's official count undercounts.
