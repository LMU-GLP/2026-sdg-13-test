# Overview: keeping a neighborhood safe during extreme heat

**The challenge:** How might we help a neighborhood stay safe during extreme
heat?

This wiki holds everything the team has verified so far, built from five
source files in [`sources/`](../sources/). Five sources is a start, not a
full literature review — treat anything below without a citation link as a
placeholder, not a finding.

## Why heat, and why this counts as climate action

Extreme heat is a "climate-related hazard" under SDG 13, and adapting to it
is literally what Target 13.1 asks for: "strengthen resilience and adaptive
capacity to climate-related hazards and natural disasters in all countries"
([UN SDG 13 targets](../sources/un-sdg13-targets.md)). Target 13.3 adds
education, awareness, and early warning — which is exactly the shape of the
one program in this wiki with an independent evaluation behind it, Ahmedabad's
heat early-warning system (see
[Existing programs](existing-programs.md)).

Nationally, in the U.S., heat death rates rose for every racial/ethnic group
tracked between 2000 and 2023, with the steepest increases arriving after
2018 ([CDC national heat mortality trends](national-heat-trends.md), sourced
from [cdc-us-heat-mortality-trends.md](../sources/cdc-us-heat-mortality-trends.md)).
That national acceleration lines up with what Los Angeles County's own data
shows locally — see [Los Angeles](los-angeles.md).

## The two candidate regions

The team hasn't picked a region yet. Two are already researched in depth:

- [Los Angeles](los-angeles.md) — a place with rising ER visits and a
  documented, order-of-magnitude gap between recorded and estimated heat
  deaths.
- [South Korea](south-korea.md) — a place where two-thirds of recorded heat
  deaths are people aged 60+, many working alone in agriculture.

Read [Los Angeles vs. South Korea](la-vs-south-korea.md) before deciding —
it lays out what's comparable between them and what isn't.

## What's already being tried

[Existing programs](existing-programs.md) covers what LA County already runs
(heat alerts, a Heat Ambassador initiative, a County Heat Action Plan) and
Ahmedabad's Heat Action Plan, the most rigorously evaluated example either
region's sources reference. Any new idea this team pitches should say how
it's different from what's in that page — otherwise it's redundant with
something that already exists.

## What this wiki does not yet answer

- No source here gives a South Korea-specific government heat program with
  the kind of mortality evaluation Ahmedabad has.
- No source gives LA-specific age or income breakdowns as granular as the
  Korea source's 60+/less-than-60 split.
- Nothing here is neighborhood-level for either region — the LA figures are
  county-wide and the Korea figures are national.
