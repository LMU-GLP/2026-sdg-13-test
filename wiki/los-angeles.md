## Harm (decision test 1)

- Extreme heat is "the leading weather-related killer in the United
  States, surpassing hurricanes, tornadoes and other severe weather
  events" ([LA County DPH, 2025](../sources/la-county-dph-extreme-heat-data-brief-2025.md)).
- Officially, LA County recorded 125 heat-illness deaths across
  2015–2024 (peaks of 22 in 2018, 21 in 2020, 22 in 2022) — but the
  county itself says these figures "may underestimate the true local
  impact of heat on mortality," and a Medical Examiner study estimated
  "elevated heat may have led to as many as 1,265 additional deaths
  between 2014-2019" ([LA County DPH, 2025](../sources/la-county-dph-extreme-heat-data-brief-2025.md)).
  See [undercounting-heat-deaths.md](undercounting-heat-deaths.md).
- The undercount is visible in real time: LA County's official count for
  summer 2024 was just 2 heat deaths, which UCLA's Dr. David Eisenman
  calls implausible — "we would expect to have more than just two per
  year" ([ABC7, 2024](../sources/abc7-socal-heat-deaths-undercounted-2024.md)).
- Heat illness is rising: ER visits for heat-related illness averaged
  898/year (2005–2022), from a low of 456 in 2005 to a high of 1,352 in
  2018; hospital admissions averaged 196/year, and both show "an
  increasing trend" ([LA County DPH, 2025](../sources/la-county-dph-extreme-heat-data-brief-2025.md)).
- Older adults (65+) are named among the groups "at greater risk" of
  heat-related illness, being "more sensitive to heat"
  ([LA County DPH, 2025](../sources/la-county-dph-extreme-heat-data-brief-2025.md));
  the CDC notes older adults are "more likely to have chronic health
  issues or take medications that affect the body's response to heat"
  ([LAist, 2024](../sources/laist-ladwp-free-ac-seniors-2024.md)).
- The danger is indoors: it "only needs to get up to about 85 degrees
  inside your home or apartment" sustained for a day or two to become
  extremely dangerous ([ABC7, 2024](../sources/abc7-socal-heat-deaths-undercounted-2024.md)).
- The exposure window is widening: "the average time from the first to
  the last heat wave of the season has increased by 46 days since the
  1960s," and July's ER surge (4,020 visits, 2005–2022, the highest of
  any month) hits before bodies acclimatize
  ([LA County DPH, 2025](../sources/la-county-dph-extreme-heat-data-brief-2025.md)).

### Neighborhood disparities

- "One of the biggest trouble spots is South Los Angeles… there are more
  homes that lack air conditioning." Leimert Park has "more than double
  the number of hospital visits than neighboring Culver City," and
  historically redlined areas (Inglewood, Compton, the ports) show "very
  high rates of heat-related injuries"
  ([ABC7, 2024](../sources/abc7-socal-heat-deaths-undercounted-2024.md)).

## Existing responses and their gaps (decision test 2)

- **Free AC for seniors:** LADWP and the City's Department of Aging give
  free portable AC units to residents 60+, on fixed low income, lacking
  sufficient AC and enrolled in LADWP discount programs. Scale: ~400
  units at one event, over 500 across the summer of 2024
  ([LAist, 2024](../sources/laist-ladwp-free-ac-seniors-2024.md)).
  - **Gap:** hundreds of units per summer against thousands of annual
    heat-illness ER visits
    ([LA County DPH, 2025](../sources/la-county-dph-extreme-heat-data-brief-2025.md)),
    and seniors must have utility-account paperwork and physically show
    up at a senior center to receive one
    ([LAist, 2024](../sources/laist-ladwp-free-ac-seniors-2024.md)).
- **County programs:** heat alerts available "by subscription… by text
  message or email," a school heat toolkit, a Heat Ambassador
  community-health-worker initiative, and the County Heat Action Plan
  ([LA County DPH, 2025](../sources/la-county-dph-extreme-heat-data-brief-2025.md)).
  - **Gap:** alerts are opt-in — the isolated elderly people at highest
    risk must subscribe. Whether they do is unknown. *(uncited — open
    question)*

## Data availability (decision test 3)

- Strong county-level surveillance exists and is public: extreme heat
  days back to 1922, ER/hospitalization series 2005–2022 (HCAI), and
  mortality 2005–2024 ([LA County DPH, 2025](../sources/la-county-dph-extreme-heat-data-brief-2025.md)).
- Some neighborhood-level evidence exists (Leimert Park vs Culver City
  hospital-visit contrast; redlined-area injury rates)
  ([ABC7, 2024](../sources/abc7-socal-heat-deaths-undercounted-2024.md)),
  but we do not yet hold a neighborhood-granular dataset ourselves.
