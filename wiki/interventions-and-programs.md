## Infrastructure: LA's Cool Streets program

StreetsLA's Cool Streets program coats asphalt with reflective sealant.
Since starting in 2017 it has covered "over 175 lane miles across Los
Angeles, covering nearly 600 street segments," funded $2–4 million per
fiscal year (Street Damage Restoration Fee and state Senate Bill 1 funds),
with named expansion areas of Sylmar, Boyle Heights, and Fairfax. Measured
effect: pavement temperature drops "by between 4 to 8 degrees" depending on
how recently it was applied
([Cool Streets LA program](../sources/la-cool-streets-cool-pavement-program.md)).
Caveat, from the same report: a UCLA study found the treatment "might feel
hotter" for a person standing on it, since it re-radiates heat rather than
absorbing it — a reminder that a surface-temperature win is not
automatically a felt-experience win
([Cool Streets LA program](../sources/la-cool-streets-cool-pavement-program.md)).

## County programs (LA)

LA County's own list: heat alerts by text/email subscription, a school
toolkit, a Heat Ambassador community-health-worker initiative (LARC-HEAT
grant), and the County Heat Action Plan
([LA County DPH data brief](../sources/la-county-dph-extreme-heat-data-brief-2025.md)).
The brief does not give measured outcomes (e.g., deaths or ED visits
averted) for any of these — unlike the Cool Streets pavement-temperature
figures, these are named but not quantified in our sources.

## A response plan with a measured outcome: Milwaukee (via EPA documentation)

The clearest quantified before/after in our sources isn't LA or Korea —
it's Milwaukee, cited inside EPA's heat-death documentation. After a 1995
heat wave, Milwaukee built a heat-emergency response plan; "during a 1999
heat wave, this plan cut heat-related deaths nearly in half compared with
what was expected"
([EPA heat-related deaths indicator](../sources/epa-heat-related-deaths-indicator-2024.md)).
This is the one intervention in our source set with a direct
before/after mortality claim, which makes it a useful reference point even
though it's neither an LA nor a Korea example.

## Behavioral guidance (South Korea)

KDCA's guidance to agricultural workers is the one Korea-side intervention
in our sources: "work in early mornings or late evenings and avoid going
outdoors in the hours between noon and 5 p.m."
([Korea Times / KDCA report](../sources/korea-times-heat-deaths-elderly-2025.md)).
It's guidance, not infrastructure or a funded program — we have no source
describing a Korean equivalent to Cool Streets or a Heat Ambassador model.

## Gap for the team

Across five sources, the only interventions with a quantified effect are
LA's Cool Streets pavement-cooling numbers and Milwaukee's mortality
reduction. Nothing in our source set quantifies the county programs
(alerts, school toolkit, Heat Ambassadors) or the Korean guidance. If the
team's concept depends on one of those working, that claim would need a
new source before it goes in the wiki, per CLAUDE.md rule 1.
