# Concept

Built from [problem-statement.md](problem-statement.md): elderly people in
South Korea who farm alone are dying of heat, in a pattern that recurs
every season — 5 of 29 heat deaths in the 2025 season through
mid-September were elderly people who died doing outdoor field work
([korea-heat-deaths-2025-season-elderly-farmworkers.md](../sources/korea-heat-deaths-2025-season-elderly-farmworkers.md)),
inside a 13-year pattern where 65%+ of Korea's 238 recorded heat deaths are
age 60+ and 32% happen on farms
([korea-heat-deaths-elderly.md](../sources/korea-heat-deaths-elderly.md)).
Official advice to avoid outdoor work between noon and 5 p.m. assumes
someone else can cover that work — advice that doesn't fit a farmer with no
one else nearby ([korea-heat-deaths-elderly.md](../sources/korea-heat-deaths-elderly.md)).

## What it is

An early-warning and check-in service for elderly farmers who work alone,
sold as a business to local governments and agricultural cooperatives
rather than to farmers directly.

## Who it's for

Three parties, all notified together:

1. **The elderly farmer** — receives the forecast warning and is prompted
   to check in during the danger window.
2. **A designated "buddy"** — a family member or neighbor, notified if the
   farmer doesn't check in.
3. **A local health worker or official** — part of the notification chain,
   following the model of trained frontline and community health workers
   in Ahmedabad's plan
   ([ahmedabad-heat-action-plan.md](../sources/ahmedabad-heat-action-plan.md)),
   who can escalate to an actual welfare check.

## What it actually does

- **Forecast stage:** Up to several days ahead of dangerous heat, a
  warning goes out to all three parties — modeled on Ahmedabad's
  color-coded, up-to-5-day early warning system
  ([ahmedabad-heat-action-plan.md](../sources/ahmedabad-heat-action-plan.md)).
- **Real-time stage:** During the noon–5 p.m. danger window on hot days,
  the farmer is prompted for a scheduled check-in (e.g., at set times).
  If they don't respond, the buddy and health worker are notified to check
  on them — this is also the fallback for a farmer who is physically unable
  to respond, not just one who forgets.

## How it works, technically

Two channels, chosen because rural elderly users can't be assumed to have
smartphones:

- **Phone calls and SMS** for the forecast warning and the check-in prompts
  — works on any basic phone.
- **A simple physical device** (a button, or a wearable) the farmer carries
  in the field, used to check in and to relay the forecast warning without
  needing a phone in hand.

## Business model and rollout

Sold to local governments and agricultural cooperatives, not to individual
farmers — putting the cost on institutions rather than the population most
at risk. Starts as **a single pilot village or township**, in partnership
with one agricultural cooperative, before any wider rollout — the same
single-city-first pattern Ahmedabad's plan followed before it spread to
other Indian cities
([ahmedabad-heat-action-plan.md](../sources/ahmedabad-heat-action-plan.md)).

## What we're deliberately leaving out

- **Emergency response itself.** The service alerts and prompts a welfare
  check; it does not dispatch ambulances or provide medical care. That
  stays with existing emergency services.
- **Non-agricultural elderly heat deaths.** Korea's data shows 15% of
  recorded deaths happen at home and 14% near roads
  ([korea-heat-deaths-elderly.md](../sources/korea-heat-deaths-elderly.md)).
  This concept doesn't address either — only elderly people working alone
  in fields.

## What we don't know yet — assumptions this concept rests on

None of our sources address these, so they're flagged as open risk, not
fact:

- Whether cell/phone reception is reliable in the fields where these deaths
  happen — no source confirms this.
- Whether elderly farmers would carry or wear a physical device
  consistently, or whether cost/reliability makes SMS-only more realistic
  in practice.
- Whether a "buddy" (family member or neighbor) is actually available and
  willing for every farmer in the target population — the whole premise of
  the problem statement is that some of these farmers work with no one
  else nearby, so the buddy role may not be fillable for the highest-risk
  cases.
- No source in this wiki describes an existing Korean program of this kind
  — see [Gaps and open questions](gaps-and-open-questions.md) — so we don't
  know if something like this has already been tried and failed there.
