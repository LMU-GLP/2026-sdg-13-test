# Root causes

Why the problem exists at all. A neighborhood isn't dangerous in a heatwave by accident —
it's the compound of built environment, housing, energy economics, and social isolation.

## Cause map (starter, *[unsourced]* — validate each link with sources)

- **Urban heat island / built environment** — low tree canopy, dark pavement, dense
  low-rise housing make some neighborhoods several degrees hotter; in LA this tracks
  historical redlining. → hotter homes, hotter streets.
- **Housing quality** — old buildings, top-floor and rooftop units, Korea's semi-basement
  (banjiha) and jjokbang housing, mobile homes: poor insulation, no cross-ventilation,
  no AC. → indoor temperatures exceed outdoor.
- **Energy poverty** — owning AC but not running it because of cost or fear of the bill;
  summer disconnection risk. → cooling exists but is rationed.
- **Social isolation** — elderly people living alone, thinning family networks (both
  regions), neighbors who don't know each other. → nobody notices distress until too late.
- **Risk perception** — heat is a "silent" disaster; no wreckage, no siren. Elderly people
  and officials both underestimate it. → warnings don't change behavior.
