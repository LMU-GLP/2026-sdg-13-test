# Comparing Los Angeles and South Korea

Sources: [LA County DPH data brief](../sources/la-county-dph-extreme-heat-data-brief-2025.md),
[Korea Times / KDCA report](../sources/korea-times-heat-deaths-elderly-2025.md).

This page exists to help the team pick a region. Per CLAUDE.md rule 5, we
do not compute a single "LA vs. Korea" ratio or rate here, because the two
sources do not measure the same thing the same way. The differences are
listed explicitly below so the team can weigh them directly.

## The figures, side by side, un-averaged

| | Los Angeles County | South Korea |
|---|---|---|
| What's counted | Deaths "due to heat-related illness" on official mortality records, California Comprehensive Death File | Deaths "from heat-related illnesses," KDCA heat-illness surveillance |
| Time window | 2015–2024 (10 years) | Since 2011 (14 years, cumulative), plus a 2025-to-date count |
| Death count | "125 deaths" (2015–2024) | "238 people" (cumulative since 2011); "seven deaths" so far in 2025 |
| Population base | LA County only | Entire country of South Korea |
| Elderly share reported | Not given as a percentage of deaths — only that 65+ is named as a higher-risk group | "over 65 percent" of the 238 were 60+ |
| Independent excess-mortality estimate | Yes: "as many as 1,265 additional deaths between 2014-2019" per Medical Examiner study | Not present in our source |

(Figures from
[LA County DPH data brief](../sources/la-county-dph-extreme-heat-data-brief-2025.md)
and
[Korea Times / KDCA report](../sources/korea-times-heat-deaths-elderly-2025.md).)

## Why these numbers can't be compared directly

- **Different geography, no shared denominator.** LA's 125 deaths are for
  one county; Korea's 238 are for an entire nation. Neither source in our
  set gives us a population figure to convert either into a rate per
  100,000 — the form SDG indicator 13.1.1 actually asks for (see
  [SDG Goal 13 and our challenge](climate-action-goal-13.md)). Reporting
  "LA has fewer heat deaths than Korea" would be misleading without that
  conversion, so we don't say it.
- **Different time windows.** LA's count covers a fixed 10-year span
  (2015–2024); Korea's is a cumulative count since monitoring began in
  2011 (14 years) plus a separate, still-accumulating 2025 figure. A
  cumulative count and a decade total are not the same shape of number.
- **Different age brackets for "elderly."** LA's brief names 65+ as
  higher-risk without a percentage; Korea's figure is specifically 60+.
  A direct percentage comparison would silently paper over a five-year
  age-bracket mismatch.
- **Different confidence in undercounting.** LA has a documented
  independent excess-mortality estimate (up to 1,265 additional deaths,
  2014–2019) suggesting the official 125 is a significant undercount; we
  have no equivalent excess-mortality study for South Korea in our source
  set, so we cannot say whether Korea's 238 undercounts by a similar,
  smaller, or larger margin.

## What the sources do agree on, without needing a shared unit

Both regions show the same *qualitative* pattern: heat deaths concentrate
among older adults. LA names 65+ as "at greater risk of HRI"
([LA County DPH data brief](../sources/la-county-dph-extreme-heat-data-brief-2025.md));
Korea reports 60+ as "over 65 percent" of its cumulative toll
([Korea Times / KDCA report](../sources/korea-times-heat-deaths-elderly-2025.md)).
That directional agreement is citable; a combined percentage or ratio
between the two is not, given the mismatches above.

## For the team's region choice

If the team wants a like-for-like number before deciding, the next source
to find would be one that reports both places on the same basis — e.g. a
disaster database (such as EM-DAT, which underlies SDG indicator 13.1.1)
that standardizes deaths per 100,000 population across countries. Neither
of our current two sources does this.
