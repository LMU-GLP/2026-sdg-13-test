# Extreme Heat in LA County: An Emerging Public Health Concern

- **Source:** Los Angeles County Department of Public Health, Office of Environmental Justice and Climate Health — data brief, November 2025
- **URL:** http://publichealth.lacounty.gov/eh/docs/about/heat-related-illness-data_brief.pdf
- **Read on:** 2026-07-30

## Key figures

- An extreme heat day in LA County means a maximum temperature of **90°F or higher** in downtown Los Angeles. There were **509 of them between 2005 and 2024**.
- ER visits for heat-related illness, 2005–2022: a low of **456 (2005)**, a high of **1,352 (2018)**, averaging **898 per year**. 1,195 in 2022.
- Hospital admissions over the same period: **96 (2011)** to **461 (2018)**, averaging **196 per year**.
- Officially recorded heat deaths: **125 across 2015–2024**, peaking at 22 (2018), 21 (2020), and 22 (2022). Only **34** were recorded across the entire decade 2005–2014.
- A 2024 Medical Examiner study found deaths rose **7%, 15% and 66%** on days of moderate, major and extreme HeatRisk compared with no-risk days.
- **The heat season is 46 days longer than it was in the 1960s**, measured from the first heat wave to the last.
- July produces the most ER visits (**4,020** across 2005–2022), even though August and September have more extreme heat days — early-season heat hits unacclimatised bodies hardest.

## Direct quotes

> "Extreme heat is the leading weather-related killer in the United States, surpassing hurricanes, tornadoes and other severe weather events."

> The official death counts "fall well below those for inland areas such as Phoenix, Arizona (approximately 118, 213 and 238 heat-caused deaths in 2018, 2020, and 2022, respectively), and may underestimate the true local impact of heat on mortality."

> "elevated heat may have led to as many as 1,265 additional deaths between 2014-2019"

## The gap worth noticing

The county records 125 heat deaths over ten years, and its own Medical
Examiner analysis suggests heat contributed to as many as 1,265 additional
deaths in six years. Those two numbers describe the same problem and differ by
an order of magnitude, because heat usually kills by worsening something else.
A death certificate says heart failure, not heat. That gap is itself a finding.

## What the county already does

Heat alerts by text and email subscription, a schools toolkit, a Heat
Ambassador community-health-worker initiative funded by the LARC-HEAT grant,
and the County Heat Action Plan. Check these before proposing something that
already exists.

## Summary

Los Angeles County's own public health data brief documents rising heat harm
and then says plainly that its own numbers are probably too low. Older adults
are named among the highest-risk groups, ER visits average 898 a year and
peaked at 1,352, and the season during which any of this can happen has grown
by 46 days since the 1960s.
