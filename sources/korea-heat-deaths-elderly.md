# 2 in 3 people in heat-related deaths aged over 60: KDCA

- **Source:** Yonhap via *The Korea Times*, July 9, 2025. Figures from the Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency.
- **URL:** https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/southkorea/environment-animals/20250709/2-in-3-people-in-heat-related-deaths-aged-over-60-according-to-kdca
- **Read on:** 2026-07-30

## Key figures

- **238 people have died of heat-related illness since monitoring began in 2011.**
- **156 of them, over 65%, were aged 60 or older.**
- Where people were stricken: **32% on farms**, **15% at home**, roughly **14% near roads**.
- In 2025 as of July 8: seven deaths, **four of them aged 70 or older**.

## Direct quotes

> "Two out of three people who died from heat-related illnesses were aged 60 or older, statistics from the Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency (KDCA) showed Wednesday."

> Health authorities "recommended agriculture workers to work in early mornings or late evenings and avoid going outdoors in the hours between noon and 5 p.m."

## The two cases the article names

A woman in her 80s died after being found unconscious near a greenhouse in
Jinju, 280 kilometres south of Seoul. Another woman in her 80s was found dead
in a rice paddy on Jeju Island with second-degree burns on her chest and face.

Both were elderly women doing agricultural work alone. That is the shape of
this problem in Korea, and it is a different shape from Los Angeles.

## ⚠️ Do not compare this 238 to LA's numbers

Korea counts deaths where heat illness was the diagnosis, nationally, since
2011. The LA brief counts recorded heat deaths in one county and then says its
own figure is probably far too low. The two are not measuring the same thing at
the same scale, and putting them side by side would be misleading.

## Summary

Heat kills the old in South Korea, and it kills them where they work: two
thirds of the country's 238 recorded heat deaths since 2011 were people aged 60
or older, and nearly a third died on farms. The government's advice is to avoid
outdoor work between noon and 5 p.m., which is advice for people who have
somebody else to do it.
