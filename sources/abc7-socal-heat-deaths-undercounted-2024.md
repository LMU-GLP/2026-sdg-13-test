# SoCal likely has more heat-related deaths than we realize, expert says

- **URL:** https://abc7.com/post/heat-related-deaths-more-common-southern-california-numbers-indicate-ucla-expert-says/15275864/
- **Accessed:** 2026-07-29
- **Region:** Los Angeles
- **Type:** news

By Rob McMillan, ABC7 Los Angeles, published September 7, 2024.

## Key facts

- Official heat-related death counts for summer 2024, by county: Riverside
  24, San Bernardino 12, **Los Angeles 2**, Orange 0, Ventura 0.
- Dr. David Eisenman (UCLA) argues LA's official count is a large
  undercount: "LA County has many millions more people than the Inland
  Empire, and even though it's not as hot here, we would expect to have
  more than just two per year." Deaths from kidney failure or dehydration
  often aren't officially linked to heat exposure.
- Eisenman: "Heat waves don't get the respect they deserve" — he calls
  them "the most common natural disaster."
- Indoor heat is the danger threshold: it "only needs to get up to about
  85 degrees inside your home or apartment. If you're subjected to those
  temperatures for more than a day or two without staying hydrated it can
  be extremely dangerous."
- Elderly residents appear as the face of heat illness: "an elderly person
  may come into his clinic feeling nauseous and dizzy."
- Neighborhood disparities: "One of the biggest trouble spots is South
  Los Angeles. It might not be as hot as the Inland Empire, but there are
  more homes that lack air conditioning." Leimert Park has "more than
  double the number of hospital visits than neighboring Culver City," and
  historically redlined areas (Inglewood, Compton, the ports) show "very
  high rates of heat-related injuries."

## Summary

A UCLA disaster-medicine expert says Los Angeles County's official count
of two heat deaths in summer 2024 vastly understates the real toll,
because heat-driven deaths get recorded under other causes like kidney
failure or dehydration. The risk concentrates indoors (dangerous from
about 85°F sustained) and in South LA neighborhoods where homes lack air
conditioning, with Leimert Park logging more than double the heat-related
hospital visits of neighboring Culver City.
