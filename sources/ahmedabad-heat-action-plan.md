# Ahmedabad's Heat Action Plan

- **Source:** Exemplars in Global Health (case study), citing peer-reviewed
  evaluation research and NRDC
- **URL:** https://www.exemplars.health/stories/ahmedabad-indias-heat-action-plan
- **Read on:** 2026-07-31

## Key figures

- Launched in **2013** — the first Heat Action Plan in India and in South
  Asia.
- Early research credits the plan with "averting approximately **1,190
  deaths each year**" since launch.
- The plan followed the 2010 Ahmedabad heat wave. Locals believed "about a
  dozen people died," but researchers estimated "the excess death toll
  during the single heat wave was more than **1,300**."

## The plan's four core strategies

1. **Early warning system** — color-coded heat alerts, up to **5 days'**
   notice before a heat wave.
2. **Community outreach** — "pamphlets, billboards across the city, regular
   radio campaigns, and social media in local languages."
3. **Healthcare provider training** — administrators, frontline workers, and
   community health workers trained to recognize and manage heat-related
   illness.
4. **Long-term heat reduction** — reflective ("cool") roof painting to lower
   indoor temperatures without air conditioning.

## Direct quotes

> "averting approximately 1,190 deaths each year"

> "the excess death toll during the single heat wave was more than 1,300"

## Do not compare this 1,190 to LA's or Korea's death counts

This figure is an estimate of deaths *avoided* by an intervention, not a
recorded death count like the LA County or Korea figures. It is also a
different city, at a different scale of exposure (dense, low-AC-access,
tropical), so it can't be read as "how many lives a program like this would
save in LA or Korea."

## Why this one

The team's challenge asks how to keep a neighborhood safe during extreme
heat. Ahmedabad's plan is the most-cited example of a low-cost,
already-running program doing exactly that — five days of warning, outreach
in local languages, trained health workers, and physical changes to roofs —
and it has an independent mortality evaluation behind it, which the LA
County brief itself lacks for its own programs (see
[LA County heat brief](la-extreme-heat.md)).

## Summary

Ahmedabad's 2013 Heat Action Plan — early warning, multilingual outreach,
health-worker training, and reflective roofs — is credited with averting
roughly 1,190 deaths a year, and it was built in direct response to a 2010
heat wave whose true toll (over 1,300 excess deaths) turned out to be far
higher than what residents believed at the time.
