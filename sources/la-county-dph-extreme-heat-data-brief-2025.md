# Extreme Heat in LA County: An Emerging Public Health Concern

- **URL:** http://publichealth.lacounty.gov/eh/docs/about/heat-related-illness-data_brief.pdf
- **Accessed:** 2026-07-29
- **Region:** Los Angeles
- **Type:** dataset

Data brief by the LA County Department of Public Health, Office of
Environmental Justice and Climate Health, November 2025. Compiles county
heat surveillance: extreme heat days (NCEI downtown LA station), ED and
hospitalization data (California HCAI, 2005–2022), and mortality
(California Comprehensive Death File, 2005–2024).

## Key facts

- "Extreme heat is the leading weather-related killer in the United
  States, surpassing hurricanes, tornadoes and other severe weather
  events." Locally, an extreme heat day = max temperature ≥ 90°F in
  downtown Los Angeles.
- ER visits for heat-related illness (HRI), 2005–2022: "ranged from a low
  of 456 in 2005 to a high of 1,352 in 2018, with an overall average of
  898 per year"; 1,195 in 2022. Hospital admissions "ranged from 96 in
  2011 to 461 in 2018, averaging 196 per year"; 338 in 2022. Both show
  "an increasing trend."
- Official mortality: "125 deaths occurred due to heat-related illness
  from 2015 to 2024," peaking at 22 (2018), 21 (2020), and 22 (2022);
  only 34 deaths were recorded in all of 2005–2014.
- The county itself flags undercounting: these figures "fall well below
  those for inland areas such as Phoenix, Arizona (approximately 118, 213
  and 238 heat-caused deaths in 2018, 2020, and 2022, respectively), and
  may underestimate the true local impact of heat on mortality."
- A 2024 Medical Examiner study found deaths rose "7%, 15% and 66% on
  days with moderate, major, and extreme HeatRisk" versus no-risk days,
  and "elevated heat may have led to as many as 1,265 additional deaths
  between 2014-2019."
- Older adults (aged 65 and older) are listed among the groups "at
  greater risk of HRI," being "more sensitive to heat."
- The heat season is stretching: "the average time from the first to the
  last heat wave of the season has increased by 46 days since the 1960s,"
  and August–September account for 118 and 121 of the 509 extreme heat
  days in 2005–2024. Yet July has the most HRI ER visits (4,020 total,
  2005–2022) — early-season heat hits unacclimatized bodies hardest.
- County responses listed: heat alerts by text/email subscription, a
  school toolkit, a Heat Ambassador community-health-worker initiative
  (LARC-HEAT grant), and the County Heat Action Plan.

## Summary

LA County's own public health data brief quantifies the gap between
official and real heat harm: 125 recorded heat deaths across 2015–2024,
while a Medical Examiner analysis suggests heat may have contributed to
as many as 1,265 additional deaths in 2014–2019 alone. It also documents
rising ER visits (average 898 per year, peaking at 1,352), a heat season
46 days longer than in the 1960s, and names older adults among the
highest-risk groups — official, citable, county-level evidence for the
"harm" and "data" arms of our decision test.
