# SDG 13: Take urgent action to combat climate change and its impacts

- **URL:** https://sdgs.un.org/goals/goal13
- **Accessed:** 2026-07-29
- **Region:** global
- **Type:** policy

United Nations Department of Economic and Social Affairs, SDG 13 goal page
(targets plus figures from the latest SDG progress reporting).

## Key facts

- Target 13.1: "Strengthen resilience and adaptive capacity to
  climate-related hazards and natural disasters in all countries."
- Target 13.2: "Integrate climate change measures into national policies,
  strategies and planning."
- Target 13.3: "Improve education, awareness-raising and human and
  institutional capacity on climate change mitigation, adaptation, impact
  reduction and early warning."
- Target 13.a: "Implement the commitment undertaken by developed-country
  parties to the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change to
  a goal of mobilizing jointly $100 billion annually by 2020 from all
  sources to address the needs of developing countries."
- Target 13.b: "Promote mechanisms for raising capacity for effective
  climate change-related planning and management in least developed
  countries and small island developing States."
- Warming status: global temperatures reached "1.43°C above pre-industrial
  levels" in 2025, and "the past three years (2023-2025) have been the
  three warmest years in 176 years."
- Disaster-related deaths fell 65%, from "2.11 per 100,000 population in
  2005–2014 to 0.75 in 2015–2024" — but people affected by disasters more
  than doubled, "from 1,199 per 100,000 population in 2005–2014 to 2,445
  in 2015–2024."
- By 2025, "141 countries reported the adoption and implementation of
  national disaster risk reduction strategies."
- At COP29 (2024), countries agreed to mobilize "at least $1.3 trillion
  annually by 2035" for developing countries' climate needs.

## Summary

The official UN page for SDG 13 sets the targets our challenge sits under —
most directly 13.1 (resilience and adaptive capacity to climate hazards)
and 13.3 (awareness, impact reduction, and early warning), which is
precisely the terrain of neighborhood heat safety for elderly residents.
Its latest progress figures show warming at 1.43°C above pre-industrial
levels and disaster deaths per capita falling even as the number of people
affected by disasters has more than doubled — resilience work is reaching
more survivors, not fewer exposed people.
