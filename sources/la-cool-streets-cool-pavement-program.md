# How Effective Is 'Cool Pavement' in LA?

- **URL:** https://www.nbclosangeles.com/news/local/how-effective-is-cool-pavement-in-la/3248308/
- **Accessed:** 2026-07-31
- **Region:** Los Angeles
- **Type:** program

NBC Los Angeles news report on the City of Los Angeles's "Cool Streets LA"
cool-pavement program, run by the city agency StreetsLA, which coats
asphalt with a reflective sealant to lower surface and air temperature.

## Key facts

- Scale: the program has coated "over 175 lane miles across Los Angeles,
  covering nearly 600 street segments since it started in 2017."
- Cooling effect: cool pavement "decreases the temperature by about 8
  degrees" when first applied; that effect "goes down to about 4 to 6
  degrees" after the first year "because of just the wear and tear" —
  an overall range described as cooling "by between 4 to 8 degrees."
- Funding by fiscal year: $3 million (FY2020, Street Damage Restoration
  Fee), $2 million (FY2021, State Senate Bill 1), $2 million (FY2022,
  State Senate Bill 1), $4 million (FY2023, Street Damage Restoration
  Fee).
- Planned expansion named in the piece: Sylmar, Boyle Heights, and Fairfax.
- Caveat from research: a UCLA study found that while cool pavement lowers
  surface temperature, "it might feel hotter" for a person standing on it,
  because the reflective surface re-radiates heat outward rather than
  absorbing it.

## Summary

LA's Cool Streets program is a concrete, funded (city + state dollars),
already-running intervention with a measured effect: 175+ lane miles
treated since 2017, cooling pavement 4–8°F depending on wear. But the
same reporting includes a UCLA finding that complicates the "cooler is
safer" assumption — reflected heat can make the pedestrian experience feel
hotter even as surface temperature readings drop, which matters for any
plan modeled on this program.
