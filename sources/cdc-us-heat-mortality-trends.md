# Trends in Rates of Heat-Related Deaths Across Population Groups in the United States, 2000-2023

- **Source:** Peer-reviewed study, PubMed Central (National Center for
  Biotechnology Information / National Library of Medicine)
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12228637/
- **Read on:** 2026-07-31

## Key figures

- The study reports "increasing mortality rates across all groups, with
  steep rises since 2018."
- **American Indian/Alaska Native** people have the highest heat-death rate
  of any group tracked, **2 to 3 times higher** than other groups: **1.07
  per 100,000** in 2011, rising to **2.51 per 100,000** in 2023. That's an
  average annual percentage change of **8.7%** from 2011–2023, accelerating
  to **27.8%** a year from 2018–2023.
- **Hispanic** rate: **0.18 per 100,000** (2000) to **0.76 per 100,000**
  (2023); **28.7%** average annual increase 2019–2023.
- **Non-Hispanic Black** rate: **0.50 per 100,000** (2000) to **0.94 per
  100,000** (2023); **28.6%** average annual increase 2019–2023.
- **Non-Hispanic White** rate: **0.14 per 100,000** (2000) to **0.55 per
  100,000** (2023); **23.9%** average annual increase 2019–2023.
- Raw death counts for 2023: **1,408** non-Hispanic White, **411**
  Hispanic, **407** non-Hispanic Black, **62** American Indian/Alaska
  Native, **42** Asian American/Pacific Islander.

## Do not average these rates together

This dataset reports *rates per 100,000 within each population group*, not
national totals, and the groups are not the same size — averaging the
percentages across groups would misrepresent the underlying counts. Read
each group's rate on its own.

## How this relates to the LA and Korea sources

This is national U.S. data, not LA County or Korea-specific. It cannot be
added to or compared directly against the LA County brief's 125 deaths
(2015–2024) or Korea's 238 deaths (since 2011) — different geography,
different time window, different counting method. It's useful as background
on *who* is dying of heat nationally (older adults and specific racial/ethnic
groups, per this and the LA source) but not as a number to merge with the
other two.

## Summary

Heat death rates rose for every population group tracked in the U.S. between
2000 and 2023, with the steepest and largest increases arriving after 2018,
and American Indian/Alaska Native people dying at 2 to 3 times the rate of
any other group by 2023. The size of the recent jump — 24–29% a year across
almost every group since roughly 2018–2019 — matches the acceleration the LA
County brief describes locally.
