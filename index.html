SDG 13 · Climate Action · South Korea

They're not dying of heat.
They're dying alone in it.

HeatWatch is an early-warning and check-in service for elderly farmers who work alone during extreme heat in South Korea.

In the 2025 heat season alone, 5 of 29 heat deaths in Korea were elderly people who died doing outdoor field work.

Source: 2025-season elderly farm-worker heat deaths

This isn't a one-season problem

How HeatWatch works

Two stages: a warning before the heat arrives, and a check-in during the hours it's most dangerous.

1

Forecast warning

Days ahead of dangerous heat, a warning goes out by phone call or text to the farmer, their designated buddy, and a local health worker — all at once.

2

Check-in during the danger window

Between noon and 5 p.m. on hot days, the farmer checks in by phone, text, or a simple field device — no smartphone required.

3

Automatic escalation

If the farmer doesn't check in, their buddy and the local health worker are notified immediately to go find out why.

This warning-first design follows the same shape as Ahmedabad's Heat Action Plan, which gives up to 5 days' notice before a heat wave and is credited with averting roughly 1,190 deaths a year in that city. Source — a different city, cited here for the approach, not as a Korea number.

Who's in the loop

The farmer

Gets the forecast warning and checks in during the danger window — by phone, text, or a field device.

The buddy

A family member or neighbor, notified the moment a check-in is missed.

The health worker

A local official who can escalate a missed check-in into an actual welfare check.

What this isn't

Being honest about scope now, so the pitch doesn't promise more than it can do.

  • Not emergency response. HeatWatch alerts and prompts a welfare check — it doesn't dispatch ambulances or provide medical care.
  • Not for every heat death. It targets people working alone in fields, not the 15% of Korea's recorded heat deaths that happen at home or the 14% that happen near roads.

    Korea heat deaths, KDCA data

  • Not launching nationwide. It starts as a single-village pilot with one agricultural cooperative before anything wider.
